From Millimetres to Microns: Hysteresis Compensation of a Cable-Driven Continuum Surgical Robot

Jun 2026 – Aug 2026 · CUHK Summer Undergraduate Research Programme

Cable hysteresis was the dominant error source; compensating it cut tip error 3.0× (234 → 78 µm), and an iterative learning layer brought it to ~15 µm.

I rebuilt a 14-notch tendon-driven continuum surgical robot with stereo tip tracking and cable-tension sensing, and identified path-dependent cable hysteresis as the dominant error source. I then designed a data-driven hysteresis compensator and a P-type iterative learning controller for the 0.25 s vision latency, and showed that tip loading widens the hysteresis loop, contradicting a friction-only assumption. The compensator was validated under a preregistered protocol on a sealed multi-target benchmark; the learning layer then approached the platform’s repeatability limit.

Task-Conditioned Kinematic Coverage of Reduced-DoF Continuum Robots for Endoscopic Surgery

Aug 2026 – Present

Can a fixed-structure 4-DoF continuum arm execute each surgical gesture within position and tool-axis tolerances — and if not, why not?

I built a kinematic simulation that tests each gesture against these tolerances, attributes failures to reach or position–axis coupling, and benchmarks four comparators. Next, I plan to replace synthetic primitives with instrument trajectories from open dVRK datasets, identify the arm’s bending structure from EM tip tracking, and validate per-gesture predictions in teleoperation on a dual-arm flexible endoscopic platform.

Physically Grounded Counterfactual World Models for Explainable Motion Prediction and Planning

Dec 2025 – Present · KineWorld (AAAI 2027, advanced to Phase 2) · PHLEX (ICLR 2027, under review)

Explain a frozen predictor’s trajectories with differentiable motion mechanisms, then re-run those explanations as physically grounded counterfactual futures.

KineWorld is a pluggable world model that decodes a frozen predictor’s trajectories against a library of differentiable motion mechanisms, giving interpretable behavior attribution and counterfactual rollouts; on UniAD/nuScenes it lowered maximum yaw rate from 0.986 to 0.640 rad/s and raised mechanism self-consistency from 8.45% to 57.27%, with gains in comfort, safety and planning accuracy.

PHLEX extends the library into a predictor-agnostic framework that explains predicted trajectories through layered kinematic models, control laws and scene references, and re-runs varied explanations as counterfactual futures for ego planning. On three frozen planners (UniAD, VAD, SparseDrive) it cut collision rate by up to half and yaw-rate violations by 1.9–6.7 pp, and in agent forecasting (UniAD/MTR/QCNet on nuScenes/Argoverse 2) it reduced kinematic violations by >80% for ≤0.05 m minFDE.
